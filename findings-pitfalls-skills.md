- For deterministic speed tests, compare snapshots after the same number of fixed simulation steps, not after the same number of rendered frames or wall-clock seconds.
- `requestAnimationFrame` timestamps are milliseconds; convert to seconds once before applying speed, cap long-frame backlog, and account for discarded simulation time.
- With inventory-driven prices, quote a one-unit trade over the stock interval it crosses. A midpoint quote makes an immediate buy/sell traverse the same underlying price interval, so spread prevents same-node market-impact arbitrage.
- To guarantee a minimum spacing on a ring without a rejection loop, place one node per equal slot plus jitter bounded by `slot - MIN_GAP`; adjacent slots (including the wrap pair) then stay >= MIN_GAP apart by construction.
- For wrap-X rendering, keep the camera unwrapped and draw every object at the ring copy nearest the camera (`x + round((cameraX - x) / RING_KM) * RING_KM`). If the viewport shows less than one ring, the visible copy switches only off screen, so the seam never pops.
- A camera that smooths toward a moving anchor must wrap its delta to the shortest arc (`delta -= RING_KM * round(delta / RING_KM)`), or it swings the long way around the ring when the anchor crosses x=0.
- Cache per-node-resource prices in a typed array refreshed by the sim tick; the render loop then never evaluates `pow()` per frame, and experiments can assert on the same numbers the player sees.
- The consumer stall gate reads `stock >= 1` before draining, so a fed consumer can drain an input to just under one unit and then stalls — the frozen bar sitting at ~1 unit is the visible "needs feeding" signal.
- Hover labels are event-driven: keep all string formatting in the mousemove handler, never in the frame loop.
- A planner that needs a trip time before departure should not integrate per call: integrate the acceleration ODE once per build change into three numbers — `T` (time to reach the speed cap), `S` (distance covered doing it), `v` (the cap) — then `t(L) = L <= S ? T*sqrt(L/S) : T + (L-S)/v`. Both branches are exact at the junction and asymptotically exact for long legs; measured error over 0.5–30 km and 1–8 wagons stayed under 3%.
- Capture a waypoint inside the step that overshoots it: solve `km = v0*t + a*t²/2` for the sub-step hit time and add that, not the whole `dt`. Measured trip times are then sub-step accurate and directly comparable to the closed-form estimate at any `DT`.
- Report the *last completed* trip in the HUD (copy the trip clock to `lastTrip*` on arrival). A live trip clock shown under a "measured / planned" label reads as a completed measurement while the train is still running.
- Make resistance signed and clamp speed at zero instead of freezing it: `a = (min(F_trac, P/max(v,eps)) - c_rr*M*G - c_drag*v³)/M`. A train whose traction cannot overcome resistance then sits at `v = 0` on its own, with no stall special-case.
- Scale gravity for the visual speed band rather than importing 9.8 m/s²: at 1–2 km/s a `TRAIN_G` of ~6 km/s² puts adhesion-limited acceleration in the 0.1–0.8 km/s² range, which is what makes a heavy consist visibly crawl.
- Reserve `sim.trains[]` (array of length 1) from the first train onward; retrofitting the array later touches every render, UI, and experiment call site.
- Draw a variable-length consist around its centre: `half = (LOCO_W + W*pitch)/2`, loco at the leading end (chosen by `dir`), wagons stepping backwards from it. Mass then reads as length, and the whole sprite stays centred on the train position.
- A stub canvas context that records `fillRect` calls is enough to verify layout in node: scan the raster for a known colour and compare the bounding box with the expected geometry (consist width, cab end, dwell bar, waypoint marks). It catches mirrored or off-centre sprites without a browser.
- Do not skip a stop by distance alone. Ignoring any wanted node within `STOP_EPS_KM` dropped the node the train was still creeping up to, so the train passed it at speed (37 stops in 600 s became 0 until fixed). Skip only the node just departed, and clear that skip once the train is clear of it.
- A constant brake cannot stop exactly at a node under a `v ≤ sqrt(2·B·d)` cap. The cap curve falls faster than `B` once the train is above it, so the train always overshoots. Brake at `max(B, v²/2d)` above the cap: that is the constant-decel trajectory `v ∝ √d`, which lands on the node. A stop found late gets a harder brake, which is allowed.
- A trade-driven rate read with a short EMA is mostly ripple. At τ = 10 s the std was 2.19 cr/s against a mean of 1.4; at 60 s, about one loop, it was 0.52. Pick the window by the std, not only the mean, and make it cover one lap.
- A mean-rate check over a short window is noisy because trade is lumpy: over 900 s the EMA mean and the cash rate differ by up to 8%, over 7200 s by under 3%. Test over a long window with a tolerance that matches it.
- Keep conserved quantities in `Float64Array`. A float32 running sum that adds a constant tiny increment every step rounds the same way each time and drifts systematically (about 0.003 units/s here); the mass balance was off by 0.01 within a few minutes. Static parameters can stay float32.
- A `Int8Array` of resource indices zero-fills to resource 0, which is R1. Use `.fill(-1)` for "empty" and check the empty state at creation, not only after a reset.
- Economy-only tests should step `Economy.tick`, not `Sim.step`. Once the train trades, `Sim.step` moves stock at the nodes the train visits, and an identity that ignores the train then fails for the wrong reason.
- Modules that capture `RR.X` at load time need an explicit require order in every experiment (`rng, const, world, economy, tech, trade, train, sim`). A missing one fails inside a later call, far from the require.
- Measure a lumpy per-event rate over whole event cycles, not a fixed window. Counting money between two moments when the train leaves a stop empty removed the in-transit inventory from the sample: std per cycle 0.038 cr/s against 0.24 for a 240 s fixed window at the same build — a fifth of the elapsed time for a sixth of the error.
- Box-mean smoothing on a grid ranks edges favourably: a truncated window never averages in the neighbours the cell does not have, so a boundary cell reads as the peak. Either reflect the window at the edge or do not smooth the ranking at all (smooth inside the check, keep the runtime field raw). On a 3-plane axis the bias moved the marked optimum a whole plane.
- Do not grade candidate builds from a fixture that one of them already balanced: measuring every cell from the *current* build's equilibrium scores each candidate for how unlike the current build it is. Nor from an artificially swept one (sources full, yards empty) — that rewards whoever grabs the backlog first. Both show up as a short sample whose *ranking* disagrees with a long live run, so assert that agreement: measure the recommended build and the default one live over 20 minutes and require the same order.
- A build knob is only a trade-off while the mechanism limits the flow. Once the market limits it (consumer yards fill, their price floors, margin → 0), extra capacity is pure cost and every surface degenerates into "own nothing". Diagnose by measuring production, consumption and delivery per build; if delivery is far below production but stops still find empty yards, the constraint is somewhere else.
- Before comparing anything measured over a window, check it is stationary: plot per-segment rates for a few builds over an hour of sim. A rate that trends (1.58 → 0.1 cr/s here while the yards equilibrated) makes every window length a coin toss, and longer windows only average the trend.
- Balance resource coverage in world generation by construction — emitters dealt round-robin, and the first recipe bit of each consumer dealt round-robin too. Random assignment at 8–10 nodes left a resource with no buyer, profit exactly zero, and "the cheapest build is optimal" as a generation artefact rather than an economic one.
- A myopic trader is path dependent: the same build parked at node 0 can find a better orbit than the one already running (2× the rate here). Report the gap between the two measurements instead of hiding it, and never let a reset-start number be described as "what your line earns".
- One flow, two readouts: a τ = 60 s EMA of a per-stop cash flow swings ±1.5 cr/s and can only be a ticker, while anything compared against a minutes-long measurement needs its own longer EMA of the same flow (τ = 10 min, ±0.05). Two fields, one subtraction per step, no allocation.
- To estimate the noise of a deterministic simulation, do not re-run it: identical seeds give identical numbers. Slice consecutive windows out of one long run instead.
- A query used to *price* a trade must not be looser than the one used to *decide* it. `bestSell` quoted any consumer that needed the resource, room or not, so the train bought cargo it could not deliver; a full consist that nothing will take means no node is wanted, so no stop, forever (4 of 20 seeds, exactly 0.000 cr/s). Quote only the nodes that would accept, and let a refusal be visible: a consumer that will not take more of the abundant half of its recipe is the stall the player can see.
- Let the headless boot test read the script list out of `index.html` instead of repeating a require list: a module the page forgets, or an order that breaks it, then fails in node with a clear message rather than as `undefined` three modules later.
