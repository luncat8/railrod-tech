# 0.1.5 — optimum emergence

Parent: `0.1.0-plan.md`. Parked planner work: `deferred-plan.md`. Builds on
`archive/0.1.4-plan.md`, which put a train on the loop and gave it a margin
rule, a cash ledger and an amortised capex rate.

Goal: the best build for a world becomes a *measured* fact. The 9 × 9 × 3 grid
of (gauge, wheel, engine) is run headlessly in the sim, every cell measured as
net credits per second, and the result is drawn in the page while it is being
found. The same grid is the tuning instrument for `AMORT_S` and the cost
constants: the ridge has to sit inside the grid, not on an edge, and the
enormous engine on 4 m gauge has to lose on every seed.

## What is measured, and what "net" means

One number per grid cell: the same `NET/S` the footer shows.

```
net(cell) = (cash(end) − cash(start)) / (t_end − t_start) − capexRate(cell)
```

`cash` is the train's own ledger, so the measurement includes everything the
trade loop does: which nodes it chooses, what it pays at the source, what it is
refused at the consumer, how long it idles. Nothing is modelled separately for
the sweep — a second implementation of the trade rules would be a second
opinion, not a measurement.

**A sample is a whole number of trade cycles.** It starts and ends on the step
in which the train leaves a stop with nothing aboard. A fixed time window
truncates a lap, so it prices cargo that is still being carried: as much as a
full consist at the destination price, which is most of the noise in a loop this
lumpy. Measured at one build: std 0.24 cr/s over a fixed 240 s window, 0.04
cr/s per cycle at the cycle boundary.

A cell therefore costs one settle (the loop's first cycle, discarded so the
initial free load at node 0 is not counted as profit) plus a sample of at least
`SWEEP_MIN_S`, cut off at `SWEEP_MAX_S` if the train never empties — which
prices a build that cannot trade as the dead weight it is.

## The fixture

Every cell is measured on the same world, so the grid is a function of the build
alone. The fixture is the live world as it stands when the pass is armed — the
layout, the yards, the prices — copied once into a private `World.blank`
together with a private train. Per cell: restore `stock` from the fixture, set
the build, park the train at node 0, run.

Every cell starts from the same parked, empty state — node 0, consist empty, one
cycle discarded — because a train already mid-orbit carries the history of the
build that got it there, and then the sample measures the orbit as much as the
iron. It also means a dot is the best a build can do from a clean start, which
is why the live crosshair usually reads below its own dot.

The pass is re-armed on the events that change the question: a new seed, or a
new wagon count. It is *not* re-armed on a gauge, wheel or engine change: those
do not change the map or the consist length, and re-measuring the whole grid on
every slider tick is not affordable. The player's own build is the crosshair,
which is live and free.

A pass ends, and the next one starts when the market has gone through roughly
one cycle of its own breathing (`SWEEP_REFRESH_S`). Since the dots average over
passes with weight `SWEEP_EMA`, and each pass samples a different phase of that
breathing, the panel sharpens while the player plays: the optimum emerges, it is
not announced.

## The economy the grid is measured in

The knobs only have a trade-off if the train, and not the market, is the
bottleneck. Generation is therefore built so that a line can never move
everything the world produces:

- every resource has roughly the same number of emitters and the same number of
  hungry yards: sources hand out their resource in turn, and so do the first
  bits of every recipe. A seed then decides *where* the cargo is and *what* it
  is worth, not whether a market for it exists at all;
- sources emit several times what the best train can harvest (`INFLOW`
  0.1–0.3 units/s against a loop capacity near 0.2), and the surplus is
  overflow: lost, not stored. That is what makes throughput worth paying for;
- consumer yards are small relative to their drain (`CON_CAP` 10–28 units,
  `RATE` 0.08–0.32 per slot), so a yard that is fed drains within a couple of
  laps. A market that can be filled up and left is a market whose price signal
  has stopped working, and the loop then decays instead of breathing;
- capacity is a hard refusal, not a slowdown: a consumer with less than one
  unit of room does not receive.

In this regime a build's net rate is stationary over hours of sim time, which
is what makes a 150 s sample mean anything.

## Cost curves, and the tuning they are given

`js/tech.js` and `AMORT_S` are unchanged from 0.1.4 — the curves below are what
0.1.4 shipped, and they turn out to be the right shape. What was wrong was the
economy they are graded in:

```
v_track   = V0 · (0.7 + 0.6 g) · (0.9 + 0.2 d)
m_wagon   = M0 · (0.8 + 0.4 g) + MW d²
track/km  = K_TRACK · (0.6 + 1.4 g²)
c_rr      = C_RR0 · (1.3 − 0.7 d)
P, m_loco = P0 (0.15 + 0.85 e)^1.8,  ML (0.5 + e)
wagon, loco cost = CW (0.7 + 0.6 g + 0.8 d²),  CL (0.15 + 0.85 e)^1.5
capexRate = (RING_KM · track/km + W · wagon + loco) / AMORT_S
```

Ridge in the interior means: the throughput gain of `g`, `d` and `e` must pay
for `track/km`, `m_wagon` and `loco cost` before the grid runs out. `AMORT_S` is
the dial for that, and in the regime above it is not a fine one: 900, 1200,
1800 and 2400 s all keep the optimum off the edges on five seeds, because the
throughput curve, not the capital charge, is doing the work. 1200 s is kept
because it is the setting where the *game* reads well at both ends — the default
build clears a fair margin (mean NET +0.86 cr/s over twelve seeds) and the
largest build is under water on every seed. At 2400 s the capital charge is so
soft that the top of the grid starts to win; at 900 s most of the grid is
refusing to pay for itself.

## Panel

Top right, drawn from the sweep's own arrays; nothing is measured per frame.

- x: total train mass (loco + wagons + one full consist of cargo is *not*
  included — mass is the tare the build has to drag), y: `net` in cr/s.
- one dot per measured cell, from `mean`, faint; the best cell drawn brighter
  and ringed;
- the live build as a crosshair at (its tare mass, `sim.netMean`), with a short
  vertical line down to the dot for the cell its knobs round to. The crosshair is
  the line as it actually runs, the dot is the same build in the fixture: they
  differ, and the line between them says so;
- a progress bar for the pass.
- the y half-range only grows within a pass (`yScale`): a scale that breathed
  with every sample would move the dots while the pass filled them.

The two footer rows under `NET/S` read out the ring's current value and the cell
it sits on (`—` and `PASS n%` while the first pass is running).

## Files

```
js/sweep.js            the pass: create / arm / start / step / run, cellOf / knobAt / comboFor
js/const.js            SWEEP_* and the economy ranges above
js/main.js             arm, budgeted step, re-arm between passes
js/render.js           the panel
js/ui.js               the optimum readout rows
experiments/surface.js the checks, the tuning loop, and --map for a human
```

`experiments/surface.js` prints one line per seed (best cell and value, the
default build, the biggest build, the floor of the smoothed field, how many
local maxima the smoothed field has) and decides six things:

- on at least 3 of 5 seeds the surface has a single maximum and it is interior.
  Interiority is read on the raw `mean` — that is the ranking the player climbs;
  the peak count is read on a box mean over the cell and its 26 neighbours, with
  the window reflected at the grid edge, because counting local maxima on a raw
  grid of ±0.05 cr/s dots finds hills that are not there;
- the largest engine on 4 m gauge loses to the optimum *and* to the default
  build on every seed, and is at least a quarter heavier than the optimum;
- the loop is still earning after 9000 s + 9000 s on every seed (a margin rule
  that strands a full train is a dead world, not a bad one);
- a dot is an exact live replay: the same fixture, same build, same number of
  steps, reproduced in the sim, to 1e-9;
- the live build maps back onto the cell the ring marks (`comboFor` round trip);
- a whole pass leaves the live sim bit-identical, and turning a knob re-prices
  capital but never the market: no price, stock or ledger moves.

`--seeds=n`, `--window=s` and `--map --seed=n` are for whoever is tuning next.

## Non-goals

No auto-trader, no route planning, no competitor lines. The grid is
(gauge, wheel, engine) at a fixed wagon count, because the wagon count is a
discrete control the player already has and re-arming on it is cheap. The panel
is not an oracle: it measures the world it was armed in.
