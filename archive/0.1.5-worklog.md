# 0.1.5 — optimum emergence worklog

Parent plan: `archive/0.1.5-plan.md`. This milestone also reviewed 0.1.4, and
the review turned out to be most of the work: the sweep existed within a few
hundred lines, and then had to be given an economy worth measuring.

## Review of 0.1.4

- **Perf, as planned.** `Economy.refreshPrices` moved out of `Economy.tick` into
  `Sim.step`, after the transfers, so the price array is what the frame should
  show and a headless run that never draws does not evaluate `pow()` 42 times a
  step. `experiments/train.js` builds its scratch world with `World.blank`
  instead of generating and discarding one. Benchmark of a sim step: the
  equivalent of 2252 ns, with the sweep's step (no price cache, no clock, no
  HUD) at ~570 ns.
- **The loop could die.** Two rules in `js/trade.js` were graded on whether the
  numbers looked right, not on whether the train kept working. Over 20 seeds ×
  3600 s, 4 worlds ended with the train parked forever, cargo 4/4, cash rate
  exactly 0.000 cr/s.
  - `bestSell` quoted any consumer that *needed* the resource, room or not. The
    train bought cargo it then had nowhere to put. Now it quotes only nodes
    `wantsUnload` accepts, and the wagon frees up.
  - `wantsUnload` checked the recipe bit and one unit of room. A half-fed yard
    keeps taking the input it lacks and refuses the one it is drowning in, so a
    train that specialises in one resource eventually fills its consist with
    something no consumer will take — and with no node willing to receive,
    `wantsStop` is false everywhere, so the train never stops again. A consumer
    now also refuses a resource that is already the abundant half of its recipe
    (`stock[r] ≤ min(stock over the masked inputs) + 1`).
  - After both: 0/20 dead, late-run rates 0.06–2.2 cr/s.
- **Why 0.1.4 looked healthy and was not.** Its checks measured a few thousand
  seconds and the death took 1200–3000 s to appear, depending on the seed. Any
  liveness claim in this repo needs a window an order of magnitude longer than
  the symptom. The 40,000 s segment probe (`/tmp` scratch, since folded into
  `experiments/surface.js` as the 9000 + 9000 s check) is the version of the
  question that has an answer.
- `experiments/boot.js` now reads its script list out of `index.html` instead of
  repeating it, which is how the sweep module was found to be loadable-but-not-
  asserted: the page loads `js/sweep.js` before `js/main.js`, and the boot test
  failed the moment it checked the page's own order rather than a copy of it.

## Implementation

  comboFor`, arrays `net mean mass span mark order cell`. It owns a private
  `World.blank` + `World.copyInto` clone and a private `Train`, restores
  `stock` from a `fixture` copy per combo, and never touches the sim.
- `js/train.js`: `tareMass` (loco + wagons), the panel's x axis.
- `js/sim.js`: `netMean`, a second EMA of the same cash flow over `NET_MEAN_S`.
- `js/render.js`: the panel (dots from `mean`, ring at `best`, crosshair at

- `js/ui.js`, `index.html`, `css/app.css`: two telemetry rows under `NET/S` —
  the ring's value and the cell it marks, `PASS n%` while the first pass runs;
  badge `PROTOTYPE 0.1.5`, capability 04 `BUILD SWEEP 9 × 9 × 3`, next-step card
  `0.1.6`, intro copy points at the panel.
- `experiments/surface.js` (new): six checks, `--map`, `--window`, `--seeds`.
  It indexes the grid with its own `cellOf`/`cellToCombo` so it never grades the
  sweep with the mapping it is checking, and it implements the sample boundary
  and the fixture itself rather than calling sweep helpers, so a dot that
  "matches" is a replay and not a shared bug.
- `js/world.js`: generation guarantees, see below.

## The measurement, three rewrites

1. **Fixed window, raw grid.** 240 s per combo, `net` straight from Δcash.
   Result: 0 of 5 seeds with an interior single maximum, 4–7 local maxima per
   surface. The peak count was meaningless: the std of a 240 s window at one
   fixed build is 0.17–0.24 cr/s, which is a fifth of the whole surface's range.
2. **Smoothing.** A per-dot running average over passes plus a 3 × 3 × 3 box
   mean, with the ring marking the smoothed argmax. The peak count behaved; the
   advice did not. A truncated window at the grid edge is *credited* with the
   neighbours it does not have, so on the 3-plane engine axis the box mean
   pulled the marked cell from engine 0.5 to engine 1.0, off the brightest dot.
   Smoothing left the module (it is now a statistic the check computes itself,
   with the window reflected at the edge) and the ring marks the raw average.
3. **Whole trade cycles.** A sample starts and ends on the step in which the
   train leaves a stop with nothing aboard. A fixed window prices cargo still
   being carried: one unsold consist is ±W × price, which is the 0.2 cr/s. Per
   cycle (≈53 s) the std is 0.038 cr/s — a quarter of the old 240 s window's at
   a fifth of its length. One settle cycle is discarded first, so the free load
   a reset train gets at node 0 is not counted as profit.

## What was actually wrong with the economy

With the sampler fixed, the surface still said "build the cheapest thing": the
optimum sat at gauge 0 on most seeds, and the *whole* g axis was nearly flat
(+2.8% cruise and −7% rolling resistance per grid step, against a monotone
capital charge). Constants cannot fix a flat axis; the mechanism was missing:

- Per build, flow was 0.05–0.23 units/s while the world consumed 0.05–0.15 and
  produced 0.3–0.75. Delivery was capped by the *market*: consumer yards filled,
  their price fell to the floor, margins went to zero. A faster train then earned
  nothing extra and the only winning move was to own less iron. The n8 variant
  of the tuning run made it stark — every build negative, the cheap corner
  declared optimal, `experiments/world.js` finding worlds where a resource had
  no buyer at all and profit was exactly zero.
- So the flow has to be train-limited *and stationary*: `INFLOW` 0.04–0.12 →
  0.1–0.3 (sources emit several times what any train can take; the surplus is
  overflow, as the parent plan always said it should be), `RATE` 0.02–0.08 →
  0.08–0.32 and `CON_CAP` 40–100 → 10–28 (a fed yard drains in a lap or two, so
  it cannot be filled up and left), `CAP` 60–140 → 16–40 (a source's whole
  backlog is a few wagons, so there is no free fortune sitting in the fixture).
  The price curves and the ledger survived the move: `experiments/mass-balance.js`
  passes with no edit at all, and `experiments/economy.js` needed only the two
  explicit `refreshPrices` calls its readouts now have to ask for.
- Generation now hands out resources in turn: `fillSource` emits
  `ordinal % RES_N`, the first bit of every consumer recipe is `ordinal % RES_N`,
  and `assignKinds` floors both kinds at `RES_N` nodes. A seed decides where the
  cargo is and what it is worth, not whether a market for it exists. Before
  this, `NODE_N` 8 left two of four seeds with a resource nobody bought.
- The transient this removes is also what the *ranking* needed. A build sampled
  against the equilibrium the player's current line has already balanced is
  graded on how unlike that line it is: from a swept fixture (sources full,
  yards empty) a short sample rewarded capacity — seed 1002's optimum read
  0.677 cr/s in the dot and 0.101 cr/s over 20 live minutes. The experiment's
  `testAdvicePaysOff` is the guard against that class of error, and it now
  passes on every seed with the ranking intact.

## Tuning

- `AMORT_S` 900 / 1200 / 1800 / 2400 in the new regime: interior on 5/5, 5/5,
  5/5, 4/5 seeds and one maximum on 5/5, 5/5, 4/5, 5/5. 1200 kept (0.1.4's
  value) — see the plan for why it is the setting the game reads well at.
- Cost scaling was tried and rejected: `K_TRACK` 15–25 with `CW` 90 / `CL` 600
  (and `AMORT_S` down to 600–900 to compensate) pushed the default build below
  zero on most seeds, after which *every* surface argument degenerates into
  "own nothing". `MW` 20, `BRAKE_DECEL` 1.2, `UNITS_PER_S` 5, `P0` 260: each
  moved the ridge a step, none of them was the problem.
- `NODE_N` stays 14. At 8–10 nodes the worlds are fragile and the cruise/stop
  balance does not improve enough to matter.
- The panel's sample is deliberately shorter than the check's default
  (`SWEEP_MIN_S` 150 s, refine over passes) and the check runs the page's
  configuration, so what is certified is what is shown.

## Decisions

- The sweep measures in the sim, not in a model of the sim. There is no
  analytic surface anywhere in 0.1.5; `--map` prints what was run.
- The ring marks the argmax of the averaged dots. No smoothing in the runtime
  module, because an edge-truncated window biases toward the edges (see above).
- A knob change does not re-arm. The plan asked for the panel to be "recomputed
  on knob change only, never per frame"; the grid does not depend on the
  player's knobs at all, so what is recomputed on a knob change is the crosshair,
  which reads `sim.netMean` — cheaper than any re-measurement and honest.
- The crosshair uses `netMean` (τ = 600 s), not the HUD's `netRate` (τ = 60 s).
  A trade pays out a whole consist at once, so the short EMA swings by ±1.5 cr/s
  around a hill that is 0.2 cr/s tall: on the panel it would read as a different
  build every second. The HUD keeps the ticker; the panel gets the rate.
- `sim.netMean` is re-anchored with the capital term on a build change (in
  `refreshCapex`), so the crosshair prices a new consist at once and then lets
  the flow part settle.
- The pass rests. It re-arms only when the world has moved on by
  `SWEEP_REFRESH_S`, and only between passes, so a pass is never restarted
  mid-flight by its own refresh timer.
- `experiments/surface.js` is the tuning loop as well as the check: it took
  ~6 s per seed per pass, which is what made 20+ candidate constant sets
  affordable to argue with.

## Known limits

- **The dots are optimistic about their level, not their ranking.** Every cell
  starts parked at node 0 with an empty consist, which is the best seat in the
  house: the yards in front of it are unclaimed. The myopic planner is also
  path-dependent — a line already running can settle into a poorer orbit than
  the one a fresh start picks. Seed 1002 is the clearest case: the marked build
  reads 0.677 cr/s in the dot and 0.101 cr/s measured live, and its default
  build 0.634 and -0.007. The *ordering* is the same in both readings, which is
  what the panel is for; the gap line between crosshair and dot is what shows
  the rest. A planner that can leave a bad orbit is `deferred-plan.md` §2.
- The y scale only grows within a pass, so a first pass can leave the dots
  crammed into the middle of the plot until a larger sample stretches the scale.
- 31 s for the check is affordable but not casual. `--seeds=3 --window=60` is the
  tuning setting (14 s, over half of it the two long-window checks rather than
  the grid) and it agrees with the full run on the verdict — at a 60 s minimum
  sample the surface is still one interior hill.
