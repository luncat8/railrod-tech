# 0.1.4 — loop trade, profit rate, reconfigurable build

Parent: `0.1.0-plan.md`. Parked trading and planner work: `deferred-plan.md`.

Goal: one train runs a one-way loop around the ring, stops only where it can
trade, and the player can reconfigure it live. The HUD shows what that build
earns per second. Money is not a constraint, so the only number that matters
is how fast the balance changes.

## Movement rule

- The train always runs in the `+x` direction and wraps at the seam. It never
  reverses and has no destination. The old click-to-route is removed.
- Going around the loop, the train brakes for the nearest node ahead where a
  stop is wanted and stops there. Nodes it does not want are passed at speed.
- A node is wanted when at least one of these holds:
  - **unload:** the train carries a unit of resource `r` and the node is a
    consumer whose recipe includes `r` and whose yard has room for one unit;
  - **load:** the train has an empty wagon and the node is a source holding at
    least one unit of some `r` with a positive margin (see Trade).
- The stop is the nearest wanted node ahead. The node the train has just left
  is ignored until the train is `STOP_EPS_KM` clear of it, so a stop it is
  still creeping up to is never skipped.

## Speed control (braking to the stop)

Stops are real stops, so the train has to brake for them.

```
stopKm  = forward distance to the nearest wanted node (none → no stop)
vCap    = stopKm given ? min(vTrack, sqrt(2 * BRAKE_DECEL * stopKm)) : vTrack
if v > vCap:  decel = max(BRAKE_DECEL, v² / (2 * stopKm)) (no stop: BRAKE_DECEL)
              v' = max(0, v - decel * dt)                  // brake, no traction
else:         v' = min(vCap, max(0, v + accel(v) * dt))    // traction or resistance
```

- The brake is never gentler than `BRAKE_DECEL`, and it is harder when the
  train is above the curve. That second part matters: the cap curve
  `sqrt(2·B·d)` falls faster than `B` once the train is above it, so a constant
  brake always ends short of the node. The law above is the constant-decel
  trajectory `v ∝ √d` that stops exactly at the node. A stop found late (a new
  node appears inside the braking distance) gets a harder brake, and that is
  allowed; `experiments/train.js` measures the peak on a clean approach.
- `accel(v)` is the 0.1.3 signed force model, unchanged. A stalled build still
  sits at `v = 0`.
- `BRAKE_DECEL` is a constant, not a function of the consist: brakes are not a
  knob yet.
- Arrival: if the step covers the stop distance, the train is captured at the
  node (`x = node`, `v = 0`) and the transfer happens.
- Wanted-ness changes slowly (only stocks drift, and the train's own cargo
  changes at stops), so the nearest-stop scan runs every step over N nodes and
  never needs an event queue.

## Trade (`js/trade.js`)

Price rules are the existing curves in `economy.js`; the train is one more
party, not a special case.

- **Sell to a consumer:** one unit at a time, quoted with `Economy.sellQuote`
  (stock interval `s → s+1`), cash `+=` quote, stock `+= 1`. The unit leaves the
  wagon. Requires room for one unit in the yard.
- **Buy from a source:** one unit at a time, quoted with `Economy.buyQuote`
  (`s → s-1`), cash `-=` quote, stock `-= 1`. Requires at least one unit in
  stock. The train buys only if the margin is positive:
  `margin(i, r) = bestSell(r) - buyQuote(i, r)`, where `bestSell(r)` is the
  highest sell quote among consumers whose recipe includes `r`. This is the
  naive-planner bias the parked auto-trader would have corrected.
- Sources do not buy back, and consumers do not sell. A consumer needs its
  input to eat; selling it would starve the consumer.
- At a stop the order is: all unloads in wagon-index order, then all loads into
  the freed and empty wagons in wagon-index order. Each unit is quoted after the
  previous one has moved stock.
- Dwell is transfer-driven: `dwell = units_moved / UNITS_PER_S`. A stop that
  moves nothing is not a stop: the train departs without dwelling.
- Cargo that cannot unload anywhere reachable stays aboard. The loop reaches
  every node, so unloading waits only for a consumer that has room.

## Economy changes

- `world.produced[idx]` accumulates the inflow each tick, `inflow * dt`, before
  overflow is taken off. This is the ledger that the mass-balance check needs;
  nothing else in the economy changes.
- The train's cash is a running total on the train. Trade only adds to it.

## Train state

```
x, v          position (wrapped km), speed (km/s, ≥ 0)
state         CRUISE / DWELL
at            node the train stands on, -1 while cruising
stopNode, stopKm   the nearest wanted node ahead, -1 if none
dwellLeft, dwellTotal   seconds of transfer dwell
wagons        W, 1..8
cargo         Int8Array(WAGON_MAX): -1 empty, else resource index
cargoUnits    count of loaded wagons
cash          running total of trade cash flow (signed)
build         derived from the knobs (see Tech); replaced on knob change
capexRate     build value amortised per second, refreshed on build change
```

- `Train.reset` places the train at node 0 and runs the arrival on it, so a
  seed starts with the same trade rules as every later stop.
- `Train.setWagons` never drops a loaded wagon: the minimum is the highest
  loaded index + 1. Cargo is not lost and the mass balance stays exact. The
  slider stops at that floor until the loaded wagons unload.
- Train mass is computed from `W` and `cargoUnits` every step; no profile cache.

## Build and reconfiguration (`js/tech.js`)

The knob curves from `0.1.0-plan.md` are kept. Knobs are `g, d, e ∈ [0, 1]`,
default `0.5`, live from the footer:

```
v_track   = V0 * (0.7 + 0.6g) * (0.9 + 0.2d)
m_wagon   = M0 * (0.8 + 0.4g) + MW * d²
m_loco    = ML * (0.5 + e)
P         = P0 * (0.15 + 0.85e)^1.8
c_rr      = C_RR0 * (1.3 - 0.7d)
F_trac    = MU * DRIVE_SHARE * m_loco * TRAIN_G
track/km  = K_TRACK * (0.6 + 1.4g²)
wagon     = CW * (0.7 + 0.6g + 0.8d²)
loco      = CL * (0.15 + 0.85e)^1.5
```

Readouts in physical units, shown next to each slider:
`gauge = 0.6 + 3.4g m`, `wheel = 0.4 + 1.2d m`, `loco = m_loco t`.

## Capex and the profit rate

Money is unbounded, so capex is not a one-off charge. It is a running rate:

```
buildValue = RING_KM * track/km(g) + W * wagon(g, d) + loco(e)
capexRate  = buildValue / AMORT_S
```

- The loop is the route. Track cost is the full ring, so it is constant across
  time and changes only with `g`.
- `AMORT_S` is the horizon over which the build's value is recovered. It is a
  price of capital, and it sets where any optimum sits. Tuned in 0.1.5, not
  here; it is shown as a readout.
- A knob change re-prices the build from that moment. No charge, no refund.

Profit metrics:

```
profitRate += (flow / dt - profitRate) * (dt / PROFIT_TAU_S)   // EMA, 60 s
netRate     = profitRate - Σ capexRate
```

`flow` is the change in the sum of trains' cash over the step. The EMA is the
first-order form, so a trade of `X` cr moves the rate by `X / PROFIT_TAU_S`
and then decays; no spike handling is needed.

The window is 60 s, not the 10 s of the old plan, because one loop takes about
60 s and trade lands in lumps (a stop sells 2–4 units at once). Measured over
8 seeds, 300 s warm-up and 900 s measured, the mean is the same at every window
(1.45 → 1.50 cr/s from 10 s to 90 s) but the ripple around it falls from 2.19
(10 s) to 0.88 (30 s) and 0.52 cr/s (60 s). At 10 s the readout is mostly noise.

## Constants (`const.js`, additions and removals)

| name | value | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| BRAKE_DECEL | 0.6 | km/s², constant braking envelope |
| UNITS_PER_S | 2 | transfers per second across the train |
| STOP_EPS_KM | 0.01 | the node just left is ignored until the train is this far clear of it |
| PROFIT_TAU_S | 60 | EMA time constant for profit rate; about one loop (see above) |
| AMORT_S | 1200 | capex amortisation horizon, s (provisional) |
| K_TRACK | 10 | track cost per km at g = 0, cr (g scales it by 0.6–2.0) |
| CW | 60 | wagon cost base, cr |
| CL | 300 | loco cost base, cr |
| GAUGE_MIN_M, GAUGE_MAX_M | 0.6, 4.0 | readout range for `g` |
| WHEEL_MIN_M, WHEEL_MAX_M | 0.4, 1.6 | readout range for `d` |

Removed with the old movement rule: `DWELL_S`, `PROFILE_DT`, `PROFILE_MAX_S`.
The knob defaults and the 0.1.3 force constants stay, so the default build's
behaviour in a straight line is unchanged.

## Check

- `node experiments/train.js`:
  - the train never reverses: each step moves it forward by less than 0.5 km,
    so a backward move (almost a whole lap) cannot hide;
  - it stops exactly on a wanted node (`x = node`, `v = 0`); across 8 seeded
    worlds, no wanted node is ever passed; unwanted nodes are passed at speed;
  - a clean approach brakes at no more than `1.05 · BRAKE_DECEL`; a consumer
    placed inside the braking distance is still reached exactly;
  - a train with no wanted node anywhere cruises at `vTrack` and never dwells;
  - a stalled build sits at `v = 0`;
  - dwell equals `units / UNITS_PER_S` on load and on unload;
  - `setWagons` never drops a loaded wagon, and the floor follows the highest
    loaded wagon;
  - a knob change mid-run does not move `x` in the next step and does not raise
    `v`; after a few seconds of braking, `v` stays under the new track speed;
  - determinism over the same seed.
- `node experiments/trade.js`:
  - a unit loads only at a positive margin and is quoted one unit at a time;
  - unloading pays `sellQuote` and loading costs `buyQuote` per unit;
  - cash equals the sum of the quotes, exactly;
  - a same-node round trip loses the spread;
  - `profitRate` averages to the measured cash rate over a 7200 s window (within
    5%). Over 900 s the gap is up to 8% because trade is lumpy; it shrinks with
    the window.
  - the stop rule agrees with the transfer rule on every node of 40 seeded worlds.
- `node experiments/mass-balance.js`: over 20 seeds × 20,000 steps, per resource
  `initial + produced = stock + cargo + consumed + overflow`, tolerance 1e-6
  (float64 stock and ledger; closes to ~1e-11). Knobs and wagons change mid-run.
- `node experiments/tech.js`: the default build equals the 0.1.3 build exactly;
  `track/km`, wagon cost, and loco cost are monotone in their knobs; the
  readouts are monotone; knobs are clamped to `[0, 1]`.
- `node experiments/harness.js`, `world.js`, `economy.js`, `boot.js` still pass
  (updated for the new train API); `node --check` on all scripts; `git diff --check`.

In the browser: the train runs one way and brakes into each station that wants
trade; a loaded wagon shows its colour; `NET/S` moves when a knob moves; the
wagons slider stops at the loaded-wagon floor.
