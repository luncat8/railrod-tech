# 0.1.7 — run modes and build curves worklog

Parent plan: `0.1.7-plan.md`. Four pieces — two run modes with the profit
readouts around them, a plot behind every build knob, the gauge carrying the
cargo, and the dwell rule between count and size. The model change was the one
that moved the economy, so it was done and tuned first; the rest was built on a
balance that already held.

## C — the gauge carries the cargo (done first, and retuned)

- Cargo became a **pool of unit slots**: `cargoUnits` units over `capacity =
  round(wagons · hold(g))`, not one unit per wagon. The wagon is still drawn as
  a stack — slot rows filled bottom-up, wagons filled first — and the renderer
  keeps drawing cargo that the current gauge no longer allows, at `0.45` alpha,
  so nothing aboard can be made invisible by a slider.
- `Train.setWagons` refuses to drop below `loadedFloor`, the highest holding slot
  plus one. Cargo is never destroyed by a slider, only the consist around it.
- `WAGONS` is not a free undo any more: shrinking the consist is blocked by what
  is aboard, and the plot for the control draws the curve the player is actually
  allowed to reach.
- `Trade.canLoad` is gone. Unloading walks the slots in index order and sells
  every unit a node will take; loading tops up the best margin while there is
  room. One rule, one pass, no separate predicate that can disagree with the
  transfer.

**The first tuning collapsed.** With `HOLD_MAX = 3` the ridge sat on the wide
edge of the grid on every seed: a three-unit wagon paid for itself, so the gauge
slider stopped being a choice and the plot for it had nothing to say. The dials,
in the order they were turned:

| dial | from | to | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HOLD_MAX` | 3 | 2 | 3 units/wagon puts the optimum on the grid edge |
| `LOCO_COST_EXP` | 1.8 | 2.2 | the loco curve has to bite before the engine knob is a choice |
| `K_TRACK` | 6 | 10 | track is the cost a wide gauge has to answer for |
| `TRACK_BASE` / `TRACK_GAIN` | — | 0.4 / 3 | a wide gauge pays for its own road, superlinearly |
| `CW` | 40 | 60 | the wagon carries the hold, so it carries the bill |
| `CL` | 300 | 450 | with the exponent raised, the loco needed the scale |

After it: five seeds, one interior single-peaked optimum each, the enormous
build losing on every one. The gauge plot's `HOLD` curve is a staircase over the
wagon count, which is the whole tension in one picture — you buy hold in whole
units and pay mass for it continuously.

## B — the plots

- `js/plot.js` is pure model, no DOM: `Plot.create` allocates every buffer once,
  `Plot.fill` writes in place through `Tech.deriveInto` on a scratch build, so
  dragging a slider and watching its curve allocates nothing per frame. The
  experiment asserts the buffers are reused.
- The marker was the one thing that could have lied. It is drawn at the player's
  exact x, so the builders run one step past the curve — at the player's own
  knob — and that value is what the legend shows. Reading the nearest sample
  instead was off by a third of a tonne on the gauge plot's mass.
- The wheel gets a second chart (`a(v)` at the player's wheel and at both
  extremes) because acceleration is a curve over speed, not a number: the
  engine gives `P / v`, adhesion gives a flat line, and mass divides both.
- The engine's `P / V` line crosses the adhesion line inside the domain on every
  seed tested — past the crossing a bigger engine buys nothing the rails can
  take, and the experiment asserts the crossing rather than trusting the shape.
- Rendered output was checked with a recording canvas stub (scratch, not
  shipped): every curve, legend row and axis label inside the box, and the one
  note that overflowed — the wheel's — was shortened.

## A — the two run modes

- `Clock.runSteps` is MAX: a step budget, no time scale, no accumulator, so
  `DROPPED SIM TIME` stays at zero because nothing is being chased. Measured in
  node, a MAX frame (3000 sim + 6000 sweep steps) costs ~9 ms; the sweep budget
  is larger in MAX precisely because no frame is spent drawing.
- MAX draws nothing: `Render.draw` is called only when something other than the
  clock changed the world — boot, resize, seed, pause, a build change, entering
  or leaving the mode. The boot test counts draws and asserts zero over ten MAX
  frames.
- `SIM RATE` reports what the machine did, as an EMA of `steps · DT / real`. It
  is the only honest number at 3000×.
- The sparkline's sampling interval is `max(smooth / 8, frame / 4)`: the window
  follows the smoothing the player chose, but a 50-second MAX frame cannot flood
  a window meant to hold hours of trade.
- One consequence worth knowing: in MAX a frame is 50 sim seconds, so a 60 s
  smoothing window spans barely one frame and `NET / S` twitches. `AVG / S` (the
  fixed 600 s window the sweep's dots are measured in) is the number to read
  there, and the slider is the cure if the player wants the fast one steady.

## Checks

- `experiments/plots.js` is new: four curves, finite and bounded, monotone in the
  directions the knob contract states, the marker agreeing with `Tech` and
  `Train` at the player's build, buffers reused, and the consist followed.
- `experiments/boot.js` drives both modes, the smoothing slider, the trend, and
  a plot for each of the four controls; it needed `Main.inspect` to read the sim's
  own step count, because the step readout is throttled to the telemetry tick and
  was under-reporting a ten-frame budget by four frames.
- `experiments/tech.js` gained the hold, the hatch and the dwell rule:
  `2 × wagons` is nearly twice the dwell, `2 × hold` is a third more.
- All eight files pass, and `experiments/surface.js` still reports five interior
  single-peaked optima with the enormous build losing on every seed.

## Left over

- The plot popover is positioned from the touched control's `offsetLeft`, which
  assumes the footer is the offset parent. It is, today; a future HUD reflow
  should re-check it.
- `PROFIT_TAU_S` is gone and the smoothing is a slider, but the sweep's dots are
  still measured over their own 150–600 s window. Reading a dot against `AVG / S`
  works because both are long; it is not the same window, and the plan says so.
