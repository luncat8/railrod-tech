# 0.1.8 — better testing worklog

Parent plan: `0.1.8-plan.md`. Three pieces — the trip ledger under the train, the
fixed-length line and its meter, and the bench of N lines on one world. The ledger
went in first because both of the others read it, and the sweep was moved onto it in
the same pass so that "the sample ends when the train leaves a stop empty" has one
definition in the module that owns the train.

## A — the train's trip ledger

- `km`, `stops`, `units`, `cycle` live on the train. `km` is an odometer fed by
  `Train.advance`, so it counts the ground covered and never wraps; `stops` and
  `units` are counted in `arrive` **after** the zero-move check, because a stop that
  moves nothing is not a stop; `cycle` increments in the step a dwell ends with the
  consist empty.
- Counters on the train rather than in the meter is what makes the replay check
  possible: the live line and a headless copy read the same fields off the same kind
  of object, so "the panel is an exact replay of the screen" is an assertion about
  seven numbers instead of a claim about two implementations.
- `js/sweep.js` dropped `wasDwell` and reads `train.cycle`. The two detectors are
  equivalent step for step — the increment sits in the same branch the edge detector
  watched — and `experiments/surface.js` agrees: five seeds, one interior
  single-peaked optimum each, the enormous build still losing on every one, and the
  marked build still out-earning the default live over twenty minutes.
- `Train.reset(train, world, build, wagons)` takes a build. The order matters: the
  consist is emptied first, then the build and the wagon count are applied, so a row
  clicked on the bench arrives with the wagons it was measured with and not with the
  floor the cargo aboard would have clamped it to.
- `Sim.anchor` is the old reset's readout half, factored out so a new world, a new
  line and a new build all re-seat the flow the same way. It also removed a wart:
  `cashSeen` was zeroed against a train that had already lifted its first load at
  node 0, which put a spike of a few thousand cr/s into the first step's flow and
  into every window that swallowed it.

## B — the line

- The meter is small and dumb: a target, a clock, an `armed` flag, and the seven
  latched totals. `observe` is called once a step and only while the mode is on and
  the line is running.
- **The cycle baseline is taken at the finish, not at the start.** The first version
  compared `train.cycle` against the count at the start of the line, which ends the
  line at the finish crossing itself whenever the train had already delivered
  somewhere earlier — with the rest of its cargo aboard, i.e. exactly the in-transit
  windfall the rule exists to avoid. Arming at the crossing and taking the count
  there fixed it.
- **The length is a floor.** Measured on seed 731421, default build: a 64 km line
  ends at 110.4 km (+72%), 128 km at 174.4 km (+36%), 256 km at 302.4 km (+18%). The
  overshoot is one trade cycle, so the longer the line the closer it is to the length
  asked for — which is why the default is two laps, and why the distance run is
  reported (behind a row, and beside the live total) rather than left implied.
- Two cutoffs, because there are two ways not to finish: `LINE_CUTOFF_KM` 384 km for
  a train that keeps trading without ever emptying (the enormous build, on two of
  three seeds tested), and `LINE_CUTOFF_S` 1800 s for a consist that cannot move at
  all. The stalled one is real, not a hypothetical: `8W` on 4 m gauge with a 0.4 m
  wheel and the smallest engine has more rolling resistance loaded than the engine
  has adhesion, and it sits at node 0 with 16 units aboard — 0.0 km, 1800 s,
  −5216 cr against the trading build's +242 cr in 149 s.
- The fixture is the market as it stands when the bench is armed, and the live line
  restarts from it on every run, so the market never drifts between two
  measurements. Consequence worth knowing: entering LINE mode stops the world from
  ageing past one line. That is the mode.
- Capex is `capexRate · the line's own seconds`, so time is inside the total: the
  same track over a faster line costs less. That is what makes `LINE TIME` a
  comparison and not a curiosity.

## C — the bench

- One world copy and one train, reused row by row, restored from the fixture — the
  sweep's shape, over a distance instead of a window. Rows are picked round robin
  (`scan`), because the live row is re-queued on every slider tick and a
  lowest-index-first scheduler starves everything behind it while the player drags.
- A row whose build moves mid-run is abandoned and started again, and shows no number
  while it is pending: the total it holds was measured on the build before the slider
  moved, and a stale number beside a new build reads as a measurement.
- `measured`, `best` and `bestCaptured` are recomputed from the queue rather than
  counted. The first version counted them and drifted on the first re-queue.
- DOM, not canvas. The sweep's panel is drawn on the canvas, which MAX mode freezes
  by design — a table that has to be read while the numbers come in fast cannot live
  on a still. The rows are elements built when the row count changes and filled in
  place after that, through the same cached-string path the footer telemetry uses.
- The two instruments never run in the same frame: LINE mode hands the headless
  budget from the sweep to the bench, and the corner with it. Measured, that is free
  — a MAX frame is 6.3 ms with an idle bench, 9.3 ms with a measuring one, 11.0 ms
  with the sweep instead. A whole bench of four rows over two laps is 53,764 steps,
  68 ms in node, nine MAX frames.
- The footer bars are scaled on the rows that finished clean and clip the cut ones.
  A cut row's total is capex to the cutoff (−5216 cr against +242), so scaling on all
  of them leaves the trading rows a sliver.

## Checks

- `experiments/line.js` is new and runs in 2.7 s: sixteen checks, the load-bearing
  ones being that a row is an exact live replay field for field (net, gross, capex,
  seconds, metres, stops, units — `strictEqual`, not a tolerance), that the total
  ignores the smoothing window while `NET / S` follows it, that one build in two rows
  is one measurement (which is the fixture, tested from the outside), that a whole
  bench leaves the sim bit-identical, and that applying a row takes the wagon count
  whole with cargo aboard that would otherwise clamp it.
- `experiments/boot.js` drives the mode through the page: labels swap and swap back,
  the bench builds a row a line and measures them in MAX frames, the headline equals
  the live row, a knob change restarts the line, `+ ROW` captures, a clicked row runs
  and the sliders follow, length and row counts do what they say, the sparkline draws
  bars, and the sweep's budget is handed over and handed back. Its stub DOM needed
  two things it did not have: `document.createElement`, and `textContent = ""`
  clearing children — without the second, a table rebuilt in place looks like it
  grows forever.
- `experiments/train.js` gained the ledger and the reset-that-takes-a-build;
  `experiments/harness.js` compares `km` and `cycle` across speeds.
- All ten files pass, and `experiments/surface.js` still reports five interior
  single-peaked optima with the enormous build losing on every seed.

## Left over

- The overshoot is not a knob. Ending a line clean is the right rule and its cost is
  that the distance run is the length asked for plus up to a trade cycle. If the
  comparison ever needs the length exact, the alternative is to mark the cargo aboard
  to market at the finish — a valuation model, and a second opinion about price.
- `LINE_CUTOFF_S` prices a stalled consist, and 1800 s of it is 108,000 steps a row.
  That is under a second in MAX and it is only ever paid for a build that cannot
  move, but a "has not moved in 60 s" cut would be cheaper if the bench grows.
- The bench keeps its rows across a seed change and re-measures them on the new
  fixture, which is the useful behaviour and also a way to read a row as belonging to
  a world it was not measured on. The head names the seed; nothing else does.
- Two instruments, one corner: the swept grid is unreachable in LINE mode. Reading a
  bench row against the grid's dot for the same build is the obvious next question,
  and it needs the two to agree about what they measure — cr/s against credits.

## Next step

Put the two instruments in one sentence the player can read: a bench row's total
divided by its own seconds *is* a cr/s, so the row could carry its rate beside the
total and be read straight against the dot the sweep measured for the same build.
That is a column, not a model change — and it is the check on whether the grid and
the bench agree about anything, which the plan currently only asserts they do not
contradict.
