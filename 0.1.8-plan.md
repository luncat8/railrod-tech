# 0.1.8 — better testing: the fixed-length line and the bench

Parent: `0.1.0-plan.md`. Builds on `archive/0.1.7-plan.md` (the two run modes and
the readouts around them) and `archive/0.1.5-plan.md` (the headless measurement of
a build in the sim). Parked planner work: `deferred-plan.md`.

The draft's `better testing` section, verbatim:

> add mode fixed length rail line. so after parameter change in this mode show not
> rolling average but sum total of whole line. this make easy to compare with
> previous settings.
>
> add ability to have N same seed lines with same stations but different trains to
> compare total profit and time.

Two things are wrong with the readouts as a testing instrument, and both are about
the *window*:

1. **A rate over a window cannot be compared with the one before it.** `NET / S` is
   an EMA over 5–600 s of a flow that is lumpy by nature, and `AVG / S` is a fixed
   600 s of the same flow. Move a knob and the number that answers is a blend of the
   build before and the build after, for as long as the window is. There is no
   moment at which the readout is *the new build's*, and no moment at which it is
   finished.
2. **One build at a time.** The 0.1.5 grid answers "where is the optimum" over a
   whole parameter space, in net credits per second, at 150–600 s a dot. It does not
   answer "is the train I just built better than the three I tried before it", which
   is the question a player actually asks while tuning, and it does not answer it in
   totals: what a line earned, and how long it took.

0.1.8 adds a third instrument beside the live world and the swept grid: a **line**,
which is a fixed length of track totalled end to end, and a **bench**, which is N of
those lines on one world with one train each.

## 1. The line: a total over a distance

`LINE` is a checkbox in the top strip. In the mode, the world becomes a test track:

```
line length    = laps · RING_KM               // 1…4 laps, 64…256 km, default 2
start          = node 0, consist empty, ledger zeroed, market = the fixture
end            = the first step past the finish where the train leaves a stop empty
net(line)      = cash(line) − capexRate · seconds(line)
```

Every one of those is a **sum over the run**, not a rate over a window: `cash` is the
train's own ledger from zero to the end of the line, `seconds` is the line's own
clock, and capex is that clock charged at the build's amortised rate — so a faster
line pays less for the same track, and the time is part of the total rather than a
curiosity beside it.

**The end of a line is a clean moment.** The train has to leave a stop with nothing
aboard, the same event a swept dot is measured between, because cargo in transit is
worth something at a price the line has not realised: a line cut off mid-lap prices
as much as a full consist at the destination, and prices it differently for every
build. The cycle count the end is compared against is taken **at the finish, not at
the start** — a stop the train left empty before it would otherwise end the line
with the rest of its cargo still aboard.

**The length is therefore a floor, not an exact distance.** Every build runs at
least the line it was asked for, and then whatever it takes to finish clean.
Measured on seed 731421 with the default build: a 64 km line ends at 110.4 km, a
128 km line at 174.4 km, a 256 km line at 302.4 km — an overshoot of 72%, 36% and
18%. The longer the line, the closer it is to the length asked for, which is the
reason the default is two laps and not one. The distance actually run is reported
(behind the row, and beside the live total), so the overshoot is never hidden.

**A line that cannot end is priced as dead weight.** Two cutoffs: `LINE_CUTOFF_KM`
(384 km) for a train that keeps trading without ever emptying, and `LINE_CUTOFF_S`
(1800 s) for a consist that cannot move at all — a heavy eight-wagon consist on the
smallest engine stalls once it is loaded, and would otherwise be measured forever.
A row that hits either is marked, reads red, and carries its capex to the cutoff:
measured, `8W` on 4 m gauge with a 0.4 m wheel totals −5216 cr over 1800 s against
the trading build's +242 cr over 149 s.

**The fixture.** A line starts from a snapshot of the market — the world as it
stands when the bench is armed, yards and prices included — and the live line
restarts from that snapshot every time it is run. So the market never drifts between
two measurements, and a comparison is between builds and never between two
weathers. The snapshot is taken on the events that change the question: entering the
mode, a new seed. It is *not* retaken on a build change, a length change or a run.

**What a parameter change does.** Any build move — a knob or the wagon count —
starts the live line over from the fixture and re-measures the live row, so the
total on screen is the new build's within one measurement and not a blend of two.
The `↻ RUN` button runs the same line again; the length slider runs a new one.

## 2. The readouts: the same rows, a second meaning

Nothing moves in the footer, because two footers would be two things to learn. The
rows keep their places and change their meaning while the mode is on:

| row | rolling mode | LINE mode |
|---|---|---|
| headline | `NET / S`, cr/s, with the trend ▲▼ | `LINE NET`, cr total, with `± cr VS BENCH` |
| | ` CR/S` | ` CR · 174/128 KM` — the total and the line's progress |
| second | `PROFIT / S` | `LINE TRADE`, the gross the line traded |
| third | `CAPEX / S` | `LINE CAPEX`, the line's own seconds charged |
| fourth | `AVG / S`, the 600 s window | `LINE TIME`, seconds for the whole line |
| fifth | `OPTIMUM / S`, the swept grid | `BENCH BEST`, the best captured row |
| sixth | `OPTIMUM BUILD G·D·E` | `BEST LINE W·G·D·E` |
| sparkline | the smoothed rate over its window | one bar a bench row, the tallest the best |

The colour on the headline keeps its job — it is the verdict — and answers a
different question: green when the live line beats the best build on the bench, red
when it does not. `SMOOTHING` is disabled in the mode, because a total has no window
to choose; the check that says so is that two runs of one line, at a 5 s window and a
600 s window, total the same to the bit while their `NET / S` differs.

A latched line stays latched: the train runs on past the finish and the totals do
not move with it, so the number the player reads at the end of a line is still there
when they look up.

## 3. The bench: N lines, one world, one train each

The panel in the sweep's corner — the sweep is idle in this mode, and one measured
instrument holds the corner at a time. It is DOM rather than canvas, because MAX
mode freezes the canvas by design and a table that has to be read while the numbers
come in fast cannot live on a still.

```
LINE BENCH                     128 KM · SEED 731421
LENGTH  [────────o──]  128 KM
LINES   [──o────────]  4
SLOT   W · G · D · E      NET CR   TIME S
LIVE   4W 2.30 1.00 40    +242.10   149.1
#2     8W 4.00 1.60 60    −703.32   363.0
#3     1W 0.60 0.40 20     +29.93   203.1
#4     EMPTY                   —       —
MEASURED 3/3 [████████]  + ROW  ↻ RUN
ONE WORLD, ONE TRAIN A ROW · CLICK A ROW TO RUN IT
```

- **Row 0 is the live build**, always. It is measured headlessly, so the total
  appears within a frame of the slider moving — and the line the player watches run
  is the same computation: same fixture, same build, same start, same `DT`. The
  headline in the footer and the row in the panel are asserted equal to the bit, as
  are the gross, the capex, the seconds, the metres, the stops and the units.
- **`+ ROW` captures the live build** into the lowest empty row, so it survives the
  next slider move: that is the "compare with previous settings" the draft asks for.
  Capturing a build already on the bench reuses its row instead of duplicating it,
  and a full bench wraps onto the row it has held longest.
- **Clicking a row runs it.** The train takes the whole build while the consist is
  empty at the start of a new line, so the row's wagon count is exact and not one
  clamped by the cargo the previous build left aboard.
- **`LINES` is N**, 2–8. Rows past the count keep their builds: shrinking hides
  them, growing shows them again and measures them on the fixture being held.

Rows run one at a time on a per-frame budget (`LINE_BUDGET_MAX` 6000 steps in MAX,
`LINE_BUDGET_ANIM` 2000 while animating), round robin so a slider being dragged
cannot starve the rows behind the live one. A whole bench of four rows costs about
40 000 steps — under a second in MAX. A row whose build moved mid-run is abandoned
and started again, and shows no number while it is pending: a stale total beside a
new build is a lie.

The bar chart in the footer is scaled on the rows that finished clean. A row that
ran to a cutoff is drawn clipped in the falling colour, because its total is off the
chart by construction and would otherwise flatten every line that actually trades.

## 4. What moved underneath

The train now carries its own **trip ledger**: `km` (an odometer that never wraps),
`stops`, `units`, and `cycle` — the count of trade cycles closed, incremented in the
step a dwell ends with the consist empty. Three consequences:

- the counters live with the thing being counted, so the live line and a headless
  copy of it read the same train and "the panel is an exact replay of the screen"
  becomes an assertion instead of a hope;
- `js/sweep.js` dropped its private `wasDwell` edge detector and reads `train.cycle`
  — one definition of the event a sample ends on, in the module that owns it. The
  measured surface is unchanged: the five-seed gate still reports one interior
  single-peaked optimum per seed with the enormous build losing on every one;
- `Train.reset` takes an optional build and wagon count, applied after the consist is
  emptied, which is what makes a clicked row exact.

`Sim.anchor` is new and is the old reset's readout half: it re-seats `cashSeen` on
the trains' cash where it stands, zeroes the profit EMA and the sparkline, and
re-prices capex. A new world, a new line and a new build all call it, so none of
them is read as a trend. It also fixes a small wart on the way: `cashSeen` used to be
zeroed against a train that had already lifted its first load at node 0, which put a
spike of a few thousand cr/s into the first step's flow.

## Checks

`experiments/line.js` is the instrument's own:

- a line is a length and a total: it covers at least the distance asked for, it takes
  longer than the running time alone, `net = gross − capexRate · seconds`, and the
  gross is the train's ledger;
- it ends clean — consist empty, at a closed cycle, not at a cutoff;
- it repeats: one build on one fixture is one number, twice;
- it ignores the smoothing window, while the rolling rate does not;
- a longer line runs longer and totals differently;
- the bench is one world: the fixture holds the live stations, kinds and recipes,
  running the bench does not trade it, one build in two rows is one measurement;
- a row is an exact live replay, field for field;
- three trains are three totals and three times, and the best row is the largest;
- dead weight is priced: a consist that cannot move is cut at the time cutoff and
  loses to the train that runs;
- a whole bench leaves the live sim bit-identical, and spends only its budget;
- capture fills, deduplicates and wraps; applying a row takes it whole, wagons
  included, with cargo aboard that would otherwise clamp it;
- rows and laps are the player's, and re-measure what they change;
- the mode is a toggle: the seed, the step count and the market survive it, and the
  rolling readouts are anchored, not read as a trend, on the way back.

`experiments/boot.js` drives the page through the mode: the labels swap and swap
back, the bench builds a row a line and measures them in MAX frames, the headline
equals the live row, a knob change restarts the line, `+ ROW` captures, a clicked row
runs, the length and row sliders do what they say, the sparkline draws bars, the
sweep's budget is handed over and handed back.

`experiments/train.js` gained the ledger (forward only, a stop that moves nothing
counts as nothing, a reset zeroes it and takes the build it is given) and
`experiments/harness.js` compares the ledger across speeds.

## Non-goals

No second train on the live world — the bench's N lines are N private runs of one
world, not N trains sharing it, so they cannot interact; competitor trains remain
deferred with the planner in `deferred-plan.md`. No per-row editing of a build (the
sliders are the editor, `+ ROW` is the save), no exporting of results, no ranking of
the bench against the swept grid: the grid is a map of the space in cr/s, the bench
is a comparison of a handful of builds in credits and seconds, and the two are not
converted between.
