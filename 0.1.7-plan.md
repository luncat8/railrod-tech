# 0.1.7 — run modes and build curves

Parent: `0.1.0-plan.md`. Builds on `archive/0.1.5-plan.md` (the measured grid) and
`archive/0.1.4-plan.md` (the margin rule and the amortised capex). Parked
planner work: `deferred-plan.md`.

Two questions the previous milestones left open, both about reading the machine
rather than the market:

1. **The page runs at the speed of the animation.** 0.1.5 answers "which build
   is better" with a 150–600 s sample per grid cell, i.e. minutes of sim time
   per dot, while the HUD offers 0.25–8× of real time. Watching a build settle
   takes a coffee. The interesting readouts — net rate, its average, whether it
   is moving up or down — do not need the train to be drawn.
2. **The build knobs are read as single numbers.** Each one is three or four
   curves in tension (the draft's "plot size (performance) vs mass (cost)") and
   the player sees none of them. The panel of 0.1.5 says *where* the optimum is,
   not *why*.

0.1.7 separates running from watching, and puts the curve behind each knob on
the screen at the moment the knob is touched.

## 1. Two run modes

`MAX` is the default. `ANIMATE` is a checkbox.

### MAX — calculation, not animation

The frame loop stops being a clock with a time scale and becomes a step budget:
every animation frame runs exactly `MAX_MODE_STEPS` fixed steps (3000, i.e. 50
sim seconds a frame, ≈3000× real time at 60 Hz). No accumulator, no dropped
sim time — there is no target rate to fall behind, so `DROPPED SIM TIME` stays
at zero and the number the HUD reports is the measured one.

The canvas is **frozen** in this mode: `Render.draw` is called only when
something else than the clock changed it — boot, resize, a seed change, a pause,
entering or leaving the mode. The world, the sweep panel and the train stay on
screen as the last frame that was drawn; nothing animates. The live readouts are
all DOM: net rate, its average, the trend, the measured optimum, and the
sparkline.

The sweep keeps its own budget (`SWEEP_BUDGET_MAX`, 6000 steps a frame — there is
no drawing to share the frame with) so the panel's measurement advances in step
with the world instead of lagging a whole pass behind it.

### ANIMATE — 2×…30×

`MIN_SPEED`/`MAX_SPEED` become 2 and 30 (step 0.5, default 8). Everything the
clock does today stays: accumulator, `MAX_STEPS` (raised to 96, so a 30× frame
on a 20 Hz display is not silently dropped), dropped-time accounting,
determinism. `MAX_STEPS` is only an animation cap; MAX mode does not use it.

Pause works in both modes. Mode switching never touches sim state, so the seed
and the step count keep their meaning across a switch.

**Measured rate.** In MAX the honest readout is not "3000×", it is what the
machine did: an EMA of `steps · DT / realSeconds`, shown as `SIM RATE ×N`.

## 2. Profit readouts, smoothing and the trend

The HUD of 0.1.5 shows one number (`NET/S`, τ = 60 s) and one average
(`OPTIMUM`, from the grid). What was missing is the *direction*, and a way to
choose how lumpy the number is allowed to be.

- `PROFIT / S` and `NET / S` are EMAs of the cash flow over `SMOOTH_S`, a
  **slider** (5–600 s, default 60). One slider, one number: at 5 s the readout
  twitches with every consist sold, at 600 s it is the build's steady rate.
- `AVG / S` is the long window (`NET_MEAN_S` = 600 s) the panel's crosshair uses,
  promoted to a HUD row so the two windows can be read against each other.
- `Δ` is the trend: a second EMA over `TREND_SLOW · SMOOTH_S` (4×) subtracted
  from the fast one. Positive means the smoothed rate is climbing, negative
  means it is falling.
- Colour carries the sign: `NET/S` and `Δ` go **green while climbing, red while
  falling**, neutral inside `TREND_EPS`. The colour is the answer to "is this
  build working", so it is on the number, not in a legend.
- A **sparkline** (96 samples of the smoothed net rate) draws the same story as
  a shape. Its sampling interval is `max(SMOOTH_S / HISTORY_DIV, frameSeconds /
  HISTORY_PER_FRAME)`: the window follows the smoothing the player chose in
  animation mode, and cannot be overrun by a 50-second frame in MAX mode.

## 3. Wagon payload: hold scales with gauge

The 0.1.0 model gave every wagon one unit and let gauge touch the wagon but
never the payload, which left "cargo hold" with nothing to plot. 0.1.7 makes
hold the thing gauge buys:

```
hold(g)        = 1 + (HOLD_MAX − 1) · g        // units per wagon, 1…HOLD_MAX
capacity       = round(wagons · hold(g))       // units the consist can carry
transferRate(g)= UNITS_PER_S · (1 + TRANSFER_SIZE_GAIN · (hold(g) − 1))
dwell          = units moved / transferRate(g)
```

Cargo is a **pool of unit slots**, not a per-wagon stack: `slot[i]` is the
resource in unit `i` of the consist, `0` is empty, and the first `capacity` of
them are the ones the gauge allows. That keeps the wagon a stack to look at
(slot rows in the renderer, filled bottom-up, wagons filled first) while the
trade rules stay unit-by-unit and index-ordered, which is what the mass balance
and the quote rules were written against.

The wagon count the build can drop to is the highest holding slot plus one —
cargo is never destroyed by a slider, only the consist around it.

Loading and unloading time therefore **grows fast with the wagon count** and
**slowly with the wagon size**: a bigger wagon moves more units, but it also
moves them faster, because the hatch is bigger with the wagon. At
`TRANSFER_SIZE_GAIN = 0.5` a full swap of eight wagons costs 8 s on a one-unit
wagon and 10.7 s on a two-unit one, against 1 s and 1.3 s for a single wagon:
eight times the wagons is eight times the dwell, twice the wagon is a third
more.

A knob change never destroys cargo. If the gauge — and with it the hold — is
reduced below what the consist carries, the excess stays aboard: it is still
drawn, still counted, still unloadable, but the consist cannot top up and
`freeSpace` reads zero until it is delivered.

## 4. Build curves

One popover, above the build sliders, shown from the moment a slider is touched
until the interaction has gone quiet. Four plots, one per control; each is a
model curve, not a measurement — the panel of 0.1.5 is the measurement, these
are the reason.

x is always the control's own domain, sampled over 40 points (wagons: the eight
integers). Series are drawn normalised to their own range, with the value under
the marker and the range over the domain in the legend, so curves of different
units share one box honestly.

| control | x | series |
|---|---|---|
| `WAGONS` | 1…8 | `MASS` (tare), `LOADED` (tare + hold), `LOAD+UNLOAD` (a full consist out and in, s) |
| `GAUGE` | 0.6…4.0 m | `HOLD` (capacity × `UNIT_T`, t), `MASS` (tare, t) |
| `WHEEL` | 0.4…1.6 m | `MASS` (tare, t), `ACCEL` (a at standstill, km/s²), `BRAKE` (stop distance from line speed, km) — plus a second chart: `a(v)` at the player's wheel and at both extremes |
| `ENGINE` | 20…60 t | `FORCE` (adhesion limit, MN), `P / V` (power left at line speed, MN), `MASS` (tare, t) |

The marker is the player's own build, and its value is the model evaluated at
their knob — not the nearest sample to it — so the legend reads the same number
`Tech` and `Train` would give.

The wheel's second chart is the one the draft asks for: acceleration is limited
by the engine (`P / v`) and by mass, so it is a *curve over speed*, not a number
— the usable traction is the lower envelope of the adhesion line and the
power hyperbola, and drag closes it at the top.

`js/plot.js` holds the curve model (pure, node-testable, allocation-free: it
writes into preallocated arrays through scalar helpers in `js/tech.js`, the same
ones `Tech.derive` uses). `js/render.js` draws the box; `js/ui.js` owns the
popover element and its show/hide.

## Checks

`experiments/plots.js` is new: every curve is monotone in the direction the
knob contract states, the wagon plot's swap time is linear in the wagon count
and sublinear in the hold, the engine plot's power line crosses the adhesion
line inside the domain (that crossing *is* the "enormous engine is pointless"
story), and the plot model agrees with `Tech.derive` + `Train.accel` at the
current build — the curves are the sim, not a picture of it.

`experiments/boot.js` gains the mode switch: MAX runs a fixed budget per frame
and leaves the canvas alone, ANIMATE runs the clock at 2×…30×, both advance the
sim, and the new controls (animate, smooth, plot popover) respond.
`experiments/tech.js` gains the hold, the hatch and the dwell rule: the gauge
sets the wagon's hold, the transfer rate grows with it, and dwell follows the
count before the size.

The 0.1.5 surface check is the balance gate for §3: the grid must still have an
interior optimum and the enormous build must still lose on every seed. Hold
raises throughput by up to 3×, so the tuning dials if the ridge moves are
`HOLD_MAX`/`TRANSFER_SIZE_GAIN`, the cost curves (`CW`, `K_TRACK`, `AMORT_S`)
and the inflow range — in that order, and only as far as the checks demand.

**Where the tuning landed.** `HOLD_MAX = 3` put the whole ridge on the wide edge
of the grid: a 3-unit wagon paid for itself on every seed, so the gauge slider
stopped being a choice. `HOLD_MAX = 2` with the cost curves rebalanced — track
`0.4 + 3·g²` at `K_TRACK = 10`, wagon `60 · (0.7 + 0.6·g + 0.8·d²)` plus a hold
charge of one per unit, loco `450 · m^2.2` — puts the optimum back inside the
grid on every seed tested, with one peak and the enormous build losing badly.
The three dials that matter, in the order they were turned: `HOLD_MAX`, the
loco cost exponent (it is what makes the engine knob a choice), `K_TRACK`.

## Non-goals

No planner, no competitor, no fuel. The popover is a teaching instrument for
the knob under the cursor, not a second panel: it shows no measurement, it does
not compare builds, and it does not stay open.
